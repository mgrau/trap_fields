# Example traps

Each directory here is a project that reproduces a published trap: its geometry and the
paper's operating point (voltages, drive frequency and ion). Each project contains a STEP
file (in mm) and a `trapfields.json` holding the electrode labels, mesh sizes, solver
settings and the paper's drive.

**Opening an example**
- **GUI:** open one with Geometry → Project → *Open an example…*. The Geometry tab then
  shows a note with a link to the paper, the paper's values, and what was assumed where
  the paper is silent. Compare those values with the Trap tab, which shows the secular
  frequencies and the Mathieu a and q of each mode.
- **Command line:** `trapfields solve examples/<name>`, then
  `trapfields analyze examples/<name> --depth`.

**Where things live**
- The files are generated by `crates/tf-app/src/demo.rs`. To rebuild one, delete its
  directory, then run `trapfields demo examples/<name> --design paul|surface|segmented`.
- `reference_frequencies.py` (numpy only) computes the independent reference values
  quoted below.
- `crates/tf-app/tests/examples.rs` checks the numbers, and
  `crates/tf-app/tests/example_files.rs` checks that the committed files match the
  generators.

"Exact" frequencies below are the Floquet frequencies of the linearized motion, βΩ/2.
"Pseudo" frequencies are pseudopotential ones.

## `paul_trap`: stretched quadrupole ion trap (3D Paul trap)

**Source:** R. E. March, "An introduction to quadrupole ion trap mass spectrometry",
J. Mass Spectrom. 32, 351–369 (1997), worked example in the section *Calculations*
([doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1096-9888(199704)32:4%3C351::AID-JMS512%3E3.0.CO;2-Y)).

**What the paper states:**
- The commercial "stretched" trap: hyperboloid ring with r₀ = 1.00 cm, endcaps at
  z₀ = 0.783 cm. The endcaps were moved out 10.6 % without changing the electrode shapes.
- V = 757 V (0-p) on the ring at 1.05 MHz, U = 0, endcaps grounded.
- An ion of butylbenzene, m/z 134.

**Assumed here:** the paper doesn't say where the electrodes are truncated. The ring's
hyperboloid runs out to |z| = r₀. The endcaps are the unstretched hyperboloids moved out
by 0.076 cm and extend to the same radius as the ring.

| | Paper | This example |
|---|---|---|
| q_z | 0.450 (8eV/(m(r₀² + 2z₀²)Ω²)) | 0.4478 |
| a_z | 0 | 0 |
| q_r | (−q_z/2 = −0.225) | −0.2239 |
| β_z | 0.318 (≈ q_z/√2) | exact at q_z = 0.450: 0.3325 |
| axial frequency | 167 kHz (β_zΩ/2 with β_z ≈ q_z/√2) | pseudo 166.2 kHz, exact 173.6 kHz |
| radial frequency | not given | pseudo 83.1 kHz, exact 84.0 kHz |
| axial well depth | D̄_z ≈ q_zV/8 = 43 eV | 48–51 eV at the endcap |

- **q_z:** the paper's formula is approximate for a stretched trap, and the solved q_z is
  0.5 % lower.
- **Axial frequency:** the paper's 167 kHz is the lowest-order pseudopotential value, and
  the solved pseudopotential frequency agrees with it.
  - The paper says this value is "high by about 5 %".
  - But the exact solution of Mathieu's equation at q_z = 0.450 gives 174.5 kHz, so the
    approximation is 4.3 % *low*.
  - The solved exact frequency is 173.6 kHz, consistent with the solved q_z.
- **Depth:**
  - The paper's axial depth is the harmonic estimate. The solved pseudopotential along
    the axis reaches 48–51 eV at the endcap; the field nearest the surface is the least
    accurate.
  - The ion's lowest way out is radial, onto the ring: 17.6 eV. The harmonic estimate,
    with the same formula, is 19.1 eV.
  - Refining the mesh near the ring (h_min 0.6 → 0.4 → 0.3 mm) gives 17.59, 17.80 and
    17.83 eV. So the mesh accounts for only about 1 % of the difference. The rest is real:
    the truncated, stretched electrodes are not a pure quadrupole near the ring.

The tests also generate an *ideal* trap that is not shipped:
`trapfields demo <dir> --design paul-ideal`. It has unstretched electrodes with
r₀² = 2z₀², and ⁴⁰Ca⁺ at 200 V and 1 MHz. Inside it, the solved potential matches the
analytic φ = V/2 + V(r² − 2z²)/(2r₀²) to within 10⁻³ V for 1 V on the ring. Its q_z and
exact frequencies match the ideal trap to 0.2 %, and its depth is within 7 % of q_zV/16.

## `surface_trap`: five-wire surface-electrode trap

**Source:** N. Daniilidis et al., "Fabrication and heating rate study of microscopic
surface electrode ion traps", New J. Phys. 13, 013032 (2011)
([arXiv:1009.2834](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.2834)).

**What the paper states:**
- RF rails 400 µm and 200 µm wide, on either side of a 250 µm center electrode.
- 10 pairs of outer segments, each 1000 µm × 400 µm.
- 10 µm gaps, 5 µm thick gold.
- Ion about 240 µm above the surface.
- ⁴⁰Ca⁺, RF about 100 V at about 15 MHz.
- DC between −10 V and 15 V.

The paper gives no DC set and no a or q. It reports (horizontal, vertical, axial)
frequencies ≈ (1.2, 1.4, 0.4) MHz, and RF quadrupole axes tilted about 25° from
vertical.

**Assumed here:**
- **RF 85.9 V, not "about 100 V".** Laplace's equation means the DC set can only move
  curvature between the three modes: the sum of the three squared secular frequencies is
  fixed by the RF alone. At 100 V that sum is 33 % larger than the paper's
  1.2² + 1.4² + 0.4² MHz², so no DC set could give the reported frequencies. 85.9 V does.
- **The DC set, fitted.** It cancels the static field at the RF null and gives the
  paper's three exact (Floquet) frequencies with the motional axes tilted 25°.
  - It is the minimum-norm solution, mirror-symmetric about the trap center, and lies
    within the paper's −10…15 V.
  - Center +0.22 V.
  - Segments 1–5 (6–10 mirrored), wide side: −3.85, −3.52, −4.37, −3.70, +2.13 V.
  - Segments 1–5 (6–10 mirrored), narrow side: −3.24, −2.38, −0.63, +11.16, −2.31 V.
- The rails and the center electrode run 500 µm past the segment rows.
- No ground plane and no substrate.

| | Paper | This example | Gapless-plane model |
|---|---|---|---|
| ion (RF null) height | ≈ 240 µm | 229 µm | 226 µm |
| axial frequency | 0.4 MHz | 0.4005 MHz | |
| horizontal radial frequency | 1.2 MHz | exact 1.201 MHz (pseudo 1.186) | |
| vertical radial frequency | 1.4 MHz | exact 1.399 MHz (pseudo 1.382) | |
| motional axes tilt | ≈ 25° | 25.1° | |
| RF quadrupole axes tilt | | 16.6° | 16.0° |
| q (radial) | not given | ±0.249 | 0.259 (RF only) |
| a | not given | +0.0028, −0.0057, +0.0029 (axial) | |
| RF-only radial frequency | | pseudo 1.317, exact 1.334 MHz | pseudo 1.371, exact 1.390 MHz |
| RF-only depth (cross-section) | | 53 meV | 59 meV |
| depth with the DC set | | 38 meV (saddle ~115 µm above the ion) | |

- **Fitted values:** the frequencies and the mode tilt are fitted, so they match by
  construction. What the fit shows is that the paper's numbers are consistent with its
  geometry, once the RF amplitude is 86 V.
- **Tilt:** the paper calls its 25° the tilt of the "RF quadrupole axes". The RF field
  alone is tilted 16–17° here and in the gapless model, and no DC set changes that. So
  the paper's 25° is read here as the tilt of the motional axes, which include the DC.
- **Ion height:** the paper's stated widths put the null near 230 µm in both models. The
  paper's "about 240 µm" probably includes rounding or details not given in the text.
- **Gapless model:** it assumes an infinite grounded plane with no gaps. The solved chip
  has 10 µm gaps and no ground plane around it, so its RF confinement is 4 % weaker.
- **Depth:** the fitted DC set confines vertically less than one chosen for depth, so the
  depth is shallow: the escape is a saddle about 115 µm above the ion. An earlier
  hand-picked DC set gave at least 0.13 eV, but with a 1.8 MHz vertical mode. The DC
  null space (21 electrodes, 5 constraints) leaves room to deepen the trap at the same
  frequencies.

## `segmented_trap`: segmented four-rod linear trap

**Source:** S. N. Miao et al., "Sympathetic cooling of a large ¹¹³Cd⁺ ion crystal with
⁴⁰Ca⁺ in a linear Paul trap", [arXiv:2201.03377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03377)
(2022).

**What the paper states:**
- Four rods 14.2 mm in diameter, with r₀ = 6.2 mm, so R/r₀ = 1.145. This is the classic
  ratio that best mimics hyperbolic rods.
- Each rod is cut into end segments A and C, each 20 mm long, and a trapping segment B,
  2z₀ = 40 mm long.
- U_rf = 150 V on rods B2 and B4 at Ω/2π = 1.961 MHz.
- U_end = 10 V on the end segments.
- ⁴⁰Ca⁺. The paper reports:
  - q_Ca = 2QU_rf/(MΩ²r₀²) = 0.124 and a_Ca ≈ 0;
  - an axial factor κ = 0.12, used as a = 4QκU_end/(MΩ²z₀²) and set in the paper's
    molecular-dynamics model by matching simulated crystal images to camera images;
  - that the well "needs to be greater than 3 eV" for stable trapping. This is a general
    requirement, not computed for this drive.

**Assumed here:**
- 1 mm gaps between the segments.
- Rods B1 and B3 grounded.

| | Paper | This example | Four infinite rods |
|---|---|---|---|
| q (radial) | 0.124 | 0.1241, 0.1246 | 0.1245 |
| a (radial) | ≈ 0 | −6.0 × 10⁻⁵ | |
| radial frequency | not given | pseudo 85.7–86.0, exact 86.0–86.3 kHz | pseudo 85.95, exact 86.22 kHz |
| axial frequency | not given | 10.8 kHz | |
| κ | 0.12 | 0.038 (0.041–0.043 for 0.2–0.5 mm gaps) | |
| RF-only saddle between rods | | 3.81 eV | 3.81 eV |
| RF-only pseudopotential at the rod faces | | | 2.27 eV (harmonic estimate qU_rf/8 = 2.33 eV) |
| depth with U_end | > 3 eV (general requirement) | 1.57 eV, onto grounded rod B1 near a segment end | |
| depth with U_end, balanced ±75 V drive | | 2.11 eV, onto a rod face | |

- **q and a:** both match the paper.
- **Infinite rods:** the radial frequencies match four infinite rods to 0.3 %.
- **κ:** the solved geometry gives about a third of the paper's value. Changing the
  assumed gaps doesn't account for it (0.041–0.043 for 0.2–0.5 mm gaps). The paper's κ is
  not a field calculation: it was tuned until simulated crystal images matched the
  camera images. It therefore absorbs whatever else shapes the crystal in their model,
  such as the axial potential's anharmonicity and the Cd⁺ ions. So it need not equal the
  geometric κ of the electrodes.
- **Depth:**
  - In the paper, 3 eV is a design target, not a property of this drive. At q = 0.124
    and 150 V, the harmonic estimate is only 2.33 eV.
  - The lowest exit is onto the rod faces, at r₀, not the 3.81 eV saddle between
    neighbouring rods.
  - With the paper's one-sided drive (rods B1, B3 grounded), the common 75 V part of the
    drive cancels some of the RF field near the grounded rods. The ion escapes onto B1,
    4 mm inside the end of the trapping segment, at 1.57 eV.
  - With a balanced ±75 V drive, that weak spot is gone and the depth is 2.11 eV, close
    to the 2.27 eV rod-face value of infinite rods.
  - Neither reaches 3 eV at this operating point.

## The needle demo (`trapfields demo`, not shipped)

`trapfields demo <dir>` creates a small linear trap that the pipeline test uses. It has
four rods 0.5 mm in radius, with their axes 1.2 mm from the trap axis, and needle endcaps
starting 2.5 mm from the center. It runs ⁴⁰Ca⁺ at ±150 V and 20 MHz, with 5 V on the
needles. It is not modeled on a paper.
